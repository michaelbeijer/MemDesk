# Licence

MemDesk is **source available**, not open source. Anyone may read, check
and build its code, to see for themselves what it does with their mail,
notes and calendar. Using it needs a licence from its copyright holder.

## Free while in preview

Until licences are on sale, a free preview licence lets you use MemDesk
yourself, with your own Google account, if you:

- set up a copy for yourself from the code on GitHub, by following its
  [setup guide](../SETUP.md); or
- were given one by the copyright holder.

It does not let you pass copies on to anyone else. While MemDesk is in
preview, the logo's menu says **Licence: free while in preview**, and
nothing is checked or kept.

The preview licence ends 30 days after a notice saying so is published on
memdesk.app. Trying the [demo](https://memdesk.app/demo/) never needs a
licence.

## Once licences are on sale

- **A free trial of 14 days** starts the first time you open MemDesk. On a
  computer, a chip in the bar says how long is left; on a phone, the logo's
  menu does.
- **To enter a licence key**, click that chip, or **Licence** in the logo's
  menu. Paste the key and click **Activate**. A Supervertaler licence works
  too, while it is in force.
- **When the trial is over**, without a key, the board, the notes and the
  calendar wait behind a screen to enter one. Everything stays in Gmail,
  untouched.
- **One key, one activation per place.** Chrome (one for your Chrome
  profile, on every computer it syncs to) and the phone app and panel (one
  for your Google account) are activated separately. **Licence → Take the
  licence off this computer** gives an activation back.
- **Checked about twice a day.** Offline, MemDesk carries on for 30 days
  after the last check that got through. After that, it asks to be checked
  again (**Check again**).

## What the licence does not allow

In short, you may not pass MemDesk on to others, changed or not, free or
paid; sell it or offer it as a service; or change, switch off or get round
its licence checks. The full terms are in the [licence](../LICENSE).

Earlier versions of MemDesk were published under the MIT licence. If you
have a copy of one of those, you may keep using it on those terms.
